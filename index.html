Math Through Art

Offering innovative ways to assist in comprehension of mathematics

Math Through Art is a math program that was originally designed to support elementary school age math learners. Over the years, it has developed to also serve older students. The units and projects have been designed to help students in their understanding of the foundational concepts of mathematics. I have been developing and teaching Math Through Art for over twelve years. I have worked with numerous classroom teachers to fine tune the projects so they can best support the necessary grade level content.
Students are asked to follow very specific directions and guidelines, but also have room for the expression of their own creativity. I find that the combination of the fixed structure established by mathematical concepts and the freedom and spontaneity of the creativity of children together make incredibly wonderful and beautiful artwork. The opportunity for creativity increases student enjoyment, engagement, ownership, pride, determination to learn, and even ability to learn. Math Through Art has been very successful in helping young children grow to love math, and to see more clearly the role of mathematics in the world around them. In this way, it also helps older students understand why math is relevant, and that it can even be beautiful.
One way Math Through Art helps, is by making abstract concepts (numbers) more concrete. It challenges students to work with numbers and amounts spatially, connecting both sides of the brain. Usually children are stronger either in their number sense or their spatial ability and Math Through Art gives both types of learners opportunities to develop and excel.

Connective Geometry

Mathematical art experiences for older students

Drawing with compass and straightedge brings to light the geometric patterns and forms that lie within the construction of our world. This geometry method connects mathematical structure with art, architecture, biology, chemistry, cosmology and philosophy. It sounds complex, and advanced studies of it can be complex, but introductory studies can be simple, clear and foundational.
Nancy loves to share her passion for the art and craft of compass and straightedge geometry with beginner students of all ages. Students can explore the miraculous way overlapping circle templates hold all the information needed to draw geometrically accurate 2D blueprints of 3D forms. Models of the 3D forms can also be built for comparison. When exploring geometry in this way, learners will encounter many intriguing topics including fractals, spirals, the golden ratio, symmetry, perspective, proportionality, and dimensionality.
The shapes are universal, foundational building blocks.The patterns and proportions are ubiquitous in all of Nature including within humans. So working with them has an incredibly widespread appeal.
Some schools have chosen to bring this experience to math or art classes for enrichment. It does not tie directly to math standards like Math Through Art projects do, but it is incredible for developing mathematical mind, skills and practices, and fostering a fascination with math. It is more suited to upper elementary through high school students.

Connective Geometry Offerings

Live experiences can be crafted to suit any age 3rd grade through Adult.

Platonic Solids In 2D and 3D: Students will learn to accurately draw and build these 5 highly symmetrical, foundational beautiful forms. Nancy can also bring zometools for another way to explore these forms and educate.

Star Study: Nancy has taught classes, camps and workshops entirely based on stars. A wide variety of stars can be used to teach a wide variety of mathematical concepts and to create beautiful drawings and paintings.

Fractal Fun: Fractal patterns are so captivating and fun to draw. Simple repetition yields complex and eye-catching patterning. When age appropriate fractal drawings can be used to illustrate and elucidate exponential growth.

Spirals!: One of the most ubiquitous growth patterns in our world. Who doesn’t love spirals? Depending on age and time allowed, Nancy can teach ways to draw many different spirals including logarithmic, golden, fibonacci, pythagorean, archimedean and polygon spirals.

Video Recorded Course: A Knew Geometry Course for Kids (of all ages) Start from the beginning and go at your own pace through 12 instructional videos. Includes templates to print and build 3D models.

Nancy teaches this content in a wide variety of settings including math and art classrooms, STEMfairs, after school programs, summer camps, workshops at yoga studios, workshops in a variety of other spaces, as well as on Zoom.

About Nancy Benerofe

I have always loved and have always drawn mathematical/geometric designs.
As a Religion major at Princeton University, I deepened my understanding of the connections between art, math and science through the in depth study of mandalas and other religious symbols.
After graduating from Princeton in 1990, I trained and then was hired as an Outward Bound instructor by the North Carolina Outward School. As an instructor, I received an incredible education. I discovered the great sense of joy that I felt from witnessing children becoming empowered, especially children who struggle with and feel disempowered in more traditional settings. In Math Through Art I see a broad range of types of learners enjoying a sense of accomplishment and feeling of empowerment as they grasp the mathematical concepts and create beautiful art. At NCOBS, I also discovered the great value in experiential education. I found learning by doing to be a highly effective way for people to learn, and I now use the methodology of experiential education in teaching math through art. At NCOBS, I also learned about the facilitation of individual and group process. I developed skills to help people learn about themselves and others through analysis of experiences and being a part of a group.
Being the mother of three children has led me to rediscover my love of craft projects. As well, I am grateful to the Winooski Elementary School English Language Learners Homework Club, where I crafted one day a week at their homework club for 7 years. The beautiful and eager students there inspired me to discover countless craft projects. They provided me a “classroom” where I learned much about crafting and crafting with kids. Through this work, I developed the commitment to and the skills and practices required for crafting with recycled/reused materials. This is a very important ethic that I am grateful to be able to role model for and share with children.
For 12 years I had the privilege of working with many incredible teachers at the Essex Elementary school and several other schools around my region. I have had the opportunity to develop art projects that support/enrich a full academic year of math curriculum for the second and third grade.
Finally, I am and always will be a student of the mystery of numbers and the magnificence of nature. Since I discovered it, I have been repeatedly drawn to learn about and explore both fractal geometry, and sacred geometry. I am forever curious about the mathematical order that underlies the infinite diversity of the manifestation of our universe, and the natural rhythm that underlies the creation and expression of our world. This passion makes the work of exploring and teaching math through art to children truly exciting and fun for me.
